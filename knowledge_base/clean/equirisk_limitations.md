# EquiRisk Limitations, Clinical Scope, and Ethical Safeguards

## 1. Intended Purpose and Regulatory Boundaries

**EquiRisk is strictly an academic research and clinical decision-support research tool.** 

- **Non-Diagnostic Scope**: EquiRisk does not possess clearance or approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), European Medicines Agency (EMA), or equivalent global regulatory authorities as Software as a Medical Device (SaMD).
- **No Treatment or Prescriptive Authority**: EquiRisk outputs must never be used to initiate, adjust, or discontinue pharmacological therapy, insulin regimens, or medical treatments.
- **Pre-Clinical Screening Function**: The system is intended to assist public health researchers, epidemiologists, and community health workers in stratifying population-level diabetes risk and encouraging timely primary care follow-up.

## 2. Inherent Data and Methodological Limitations

### A. Self-Reported Survey Biases (CDC BRFSS 2015)
EquiRisk is trained on survey responses from the CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System. While BRFSS is an esteemed, statistically rigorous surveillance framework, self-reported health data has unavoidable measurement limitations:
1. **Social Desirability Bias**: Respondents systematically underreport stigmatized behaviors (e.g., tobacco usage, heavy alcohol consumption) and overreport health-promoting behaviors (e.g., daily vegetable consumption, aerobic physical activity).
2. **Recall and Categorization Error**: Self-reported height and weight frequently underestimate BMI relative to clinical stadiometer and balance beam scale measurements.
3. **Under-Diagnosis in Uninsured Cohorts**: In survey data, diabetes status relies on the question: *"Has a doctor or other health professional ever told you that you have diabetes?"* Consequently, individuals without health insurance or who experience cost barriers to healthcare may have active, undiagnosed diabetes yet answer "No," introducing label noise into the training data.

### B. Cross-Sectional Observational Architecture
The training data is cross-sectional rather than longitudinal. While the LightGBM model captures strong statistical associations between features (e.g., hypertension, BMI, age) and diabetes status, these attributions represent **correlations, not direct causal relationships**. Feature attributions generated by TreeSHAP must be understood as local statistical contributions within the model rather than causal clinical mechanisms.

### C. Absence of Direct Biochemical Biomarkers
EquiRisk operates entirely on non-invasive survey metrics to allow low-cost, population-scale risk assessment. However, this non-invasive design introduces an ceiling on predictive discrimination because the model lacks:
- Standardized laboratory glycemic measures (HbA1c, Fasting Plasma Glucose, 2-hour OGTT).
- Lipid sub-fractions (Serum Triglycerides, HDL-C, LDL-C).
- Endogenous insulin secretory markers (Fasting C-peptide, HOMA-IR).
- Genetic risk scores or polygenic susceptibility variants.

Any individual with an elevated or indeterminate EquiRisk assessment must undergo venous blood draws to verify clinical status.

## 3. Algorithmic and Demographic Disparities

Continuous auditing of EquiRisk across demographic strata reveals specific subpopulation limitations that all users and researchers must observe:

### Young Adult Sensitivity Disparity (Ages 18–34)
- **Observed Behavior**: In the held-out test cohort, the screening sensitivity (recall) for respondents aged 18–34 is lower than for older cohorts (50–64 and 65+).
- **Etiological and Statistical Cause**: Type 2 diabetes prevalence in adults under 35 is low (~14% in the survey cohort). Because younger individuals rarely exhibit advanced comorbidities like hypertension, stroke, or decades of cumulative vascular exposure, the feature signal is weaker, and the model exhibits a higher false negative rate in this age category.
- **Mitigation and Safeguard**: EquiRisk flags this cohort as `UNDERPERFORMING` in the fairness audit. Young adults with high visceral adiposity, gestational diabetes history, or first-degree family history must not rely solely on a negative screening score to rule out metabolic disease.

### Geographic and Ethnic Anthropometric Variations
- **Western Reference Standards**: The BRFSS cohort is primarily an adult US population. Standard World Health Organization (WHO) BMI cutoff categories define obesity at $BMI \ge 30.0\text{ kg/m}^2$.
- **South Asian and East Asian Phenotype**: Epidemiological research from the WHO and ICMR demonstrates that South Asian populations develop insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and visceral adiposity at substantially lower BMI thresholds (overweight $\ge 23.0\text{ kg/m}^2$, obesity $\ge 25.0\text{ kg/m}^2$). Clinicians evaluating Asian individuals must factor in this ethnic metabolic phenotype when reviewing EquiRisk outputs.

## 4. Interpretation of Conformal Prediction Sets

EquiRisk utilizes MAPIE to generate split-conformal prediction sets at a 90% confidence target:

1. **`{Indeterminate}` Sets**: When the model outputs an indeterminate prediction set (both `{No Diabetes}` and `{Diabetes}` are included), users must **NOT** interpret this as an intermediate or moderate risk. An indeterminate set denotes high epistemic ambiguity and conflicting risk features. The correct protocol for an indeterminate result is mandatory laboratory evaluation.
2. **Coverage Scope**: A 90% finite-sample coverage guarantee implies that up to 10% of cases will fall outside the conformal prediction set. Conformal bounds reflect statistical frequency under the test distribution, not deterministic certainty.

## 5. Clinical Safety and Ethical Recommendations

1. **Mandatory Physician Consultation**: Never alter medication doses (such as metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, or antihypertensives) based on EquiRisk scores.
2. **Preventive Lifestyle Window**: Individuals in the Moderate or High risk tiers should prioritize evidence-based lifestyle modifications:
   - Achieving 5% to 7% intentional weight loss if living with overweight or obesity.
   - Performing at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise.
   - Adopting a nutrient-dense dietary pattern high in soluble fiber and low in refined sugars and ultra-processed carbohydrates.
3. **Data Security and Dignity**: EquiRisk assessments should never be utilized for discriminatory underwriting, health insurance exclusions, or employment screening.
